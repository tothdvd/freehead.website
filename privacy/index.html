FreeHead

Privacy policy

FreeHead collects nothing. Everything you write stays on your iPhone, unless you switch on calendar sync.

Last updated: 9 September 2026

What we collect

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-up and no login. FreeHead has no server of its own, so nothing is ever sent to us — we have no way to see your tasks, notes, projects or contacts.

The one exception is calendar sync, which is off unless you turn it on. It is described below.

Analytics and tracking

None. FreeHead contains no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no third-party tracking code of any kind. We cannot see whether you use the app, or how.

Information the app asks permission for

FreeHead asks for three permissions, all optional, and each is used only on your device:

  • Notifications — to remind you about due items and your weekly review. Reminders are scheduled by your iPhone, not by us.
  • Contacts — only when you choose to import someone into a "waiting for" item. FreeHead reads the person you pick and copies their details into its own storage on your device.
  • Calendar — only if you turn calendar sync on. See the section below; this is the one case where information can leave your iPhone. iOS asks for full calendar access, because keeping an event in step with a task means finding that event again later. FreeHead only ever looks up events it created itself — it never reads or searches the rest of your calendar.

Declining any of these leaves the rest of the app fully usable.

Calendar sync, and what it means

If you turn calendar sync on, FreeHead writes each scheduled item as an event in whichever calendar your iPhone uses by default for new events.

That calendar may not be a local one. If your default is a Google, Outlook, Exchange or other online account, the event — including the title you typed — is uploaded to that provider by iOS, exactly as it would be for any event you created yourself. Once there it is covered by that provider's privacy policy, not this one. FreeHead never sees or touches that transfer, and still receives nothing itself.

If you would rather nothing left the device, leave calendar sync off. It is off by default, and everything else in the app works without it.

iCloud

Syncing your tasks between your own Apple devices through iCloud is planned but not yet enabled. When it arrives, the data will live in your private iCloud database, which is governed by Apple's privacy policy. We would have no access to it.

Separately, if you use iCloud Backup, your iPhone's own encrypted backup includes FreeHead's data, exactly as it does for other apps.

Children

FreeHead is a task manager suitable for general audiences. Because it collects no data at all, it collects no data from children either.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything FreeHead stores — your tasks, notes, projects and people all go with it. There is no account to close, and we hold nothing that we could erase for you.

Two things live outside the app and are not removed by deleting it:

  • Calendar events that FreeHead created, if you used calendar sync. While the app is installed it removes its own events when you unschedule or bin a task, but deleting the app leaves whatever is still there. Those belong to your calendar, so remove them in the Calendar app or in whichever account holds them.
  • Device backups. If you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer, older backups may still contain FreeHead's data until they are replaced or deleted. That backup is yours and encrypted — we have no access to it.

Changes to this policy

If FreeHead ever starts handling data differently — for example when iCloud sync ships — this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date above will change.
